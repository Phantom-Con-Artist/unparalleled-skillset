# Assets are part of the concept

Decide how the product will be seen before committing to a composition. Inventory provided photography, product models, interface code, illustrations, video, audio, fonts, logos, source data, and rights. Inspect the actual assets, not only filenames. Record whether each is verified product evidence, contextual imagery, authored illustration, or a clearly labeled demonstration.

## Choose a credible route

Use the project's own assets when they fit the concept. Author supporting imagery or models when tools, time, and the brief justify them. Use licensed contextual material honestly, without presenting an unrelated place or person as the client's. Build code-native diagrams, spatial explanations, and product interfaces when those communicate the truth better. Do not add a photo quota or ban code-native art simply because another skill does so.

An ambitious brief deserves substantial authored material, not an empty page with a generic spinning object or a handful of flat pictograms. Conversely, generating a complex cinematic still does not prove that the actual interaction can be implemented. Label a still as a still, a prototype as a prototype, and an illustration as an illustration. Never hide missing business facts behind convincing visuals.

## Keep assets in one world

Direct camera angle, scale, light source, contrast, background separation, palette, crop, and texture together. Product detail should remain legible at the size used. Retouch or regenerate inconsistent supporting assets when appropriate rather than scattering unrelated photography through the page. A single outstanding scene can anchor the experience, but it must connect to the content that follows.

For type, verify the actual font source, license and redistribution requirements, scripts, weights, and fallback. For raster and video assets, set dimensions and poster/fallback behavior. For models and textures, plan format, compression, material complexity, and the small-device route. Use existing project capabilities before installing a dependency. Follow [framework-selection.md](framework-selection.md) when adding tools.

## Complete the path with honest fallbacks

The primary message, navigation, controls, and important facts must remain available while assets load or fail. Put a useful poster, still, diagram, or static composition in the baseline, and enhance only after the scene is ready. Keep 3D scene status distinct from task status; a failed shader should not turn a valid product action into an error.

If an essential asset truly cannot be produced from the available brief and tools, ask for that specific missing item while completing independent work. Do not pretend completion, promise an unavailable rendering capability, or block everything merely because an optional effect cannot be built.

## Production routes

Follow [pipeline-2d.md](pipeline-2d.md) for flat assets and interfaces, [pipeline-3d.md](pipeline-3d.md) for spatial production, and [pipeline-motion-graphics.md](pipeline-motion-graphics.md) for kinetic type, audio synchronization and Remotion/video output. Scratch models require open-model research and inspected visual references first; Blender MCP readiness gates dependent 3D production. For available Imagen/imagegen tools and product-specific cinematic studies, use [image-generation-direction.md](image-generation-direction.md). Preserve source/license records and distinguish generated studies from verified product geometry.
