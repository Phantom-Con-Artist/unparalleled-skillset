# Human, specific writing

Write as someone who knows the product and respects the reader's time. Establish the speaker, audience, desired action, and evidence before drafting. Sound personal through accurate observation and a clear point of view, not manufactured intimacy.

## Language bans

Do not use emoji decoration, bullet-point copy, em-dash spam, breathless punctuation, title-case slogans everywhere, canned rhetorical questions, or sentence fragments stacked like an advertisement. Default to periods and commas rather than em dashes. Preserve necessary punctuation inside user quotations, code, or proper names.

Ban empty uses of “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “cutting-edge,” “next-gen,” “seamless,” “effortless,” “supercharge,” “unlock your potential,” “elevate,” “empower,” “delve,” “leverage,” and “the future of.” A technical term is acceptable when it has a precise, necessary meaning; jargon is not evidence.

Do not write “Not just X, but Y,” “This isn't X. It's Y,” “Where X meets Y,” or “Built for dreamers.” Do not mechanically make every sentence the same length. Avoid overfamiliar “Hey there,” forced jokes, fake founder voices, and vague “we care” assurances.

## Drafting practice

Give the first screen a concrete explanation of the product and a meaningful next step. Let vocabulary inform the imagery, but name actions plainly: a poetic material metaphor should not make a button mysterious. Prefer active verbs, ordinary words, contractions when natural, and varied sentence lengths. Use connected paragraphs instead of inventories of promises.

| Weak copy | More useful copy, only if the capability is verified |
| --- | --- |
| Supercharge your workflow with next-gen synergy. | See which shipment needs attention before the morning dispatch. |
| Where innovation meets precision. | Compare the scan with the original part, down to the worn edge. |
| Effortless collaboration for everyone. | Leave a note on the frame so the editor sees exactly what you mean. |
| We understand your journey. | Your draft stays saved when you close the browser. |

Do not copy these examples into unrelated projects. Derive language from the actual user's situation. Use real names, measurements, anecdotes, and product limits only when provided or verified. Never invent personal experience to make writing feel human.

## Revision and finish

Read the copy aloud once. Replace generic praise with a fact or remove it. Check whether a stranger can identify the product without interpreting a metaphor. Check CTA labels against their actual behavior, and keep errors calm, specific, and recoverable. Stop once the language is accurate, natural, and useful; do not sand every sentence into the same rhythm.
