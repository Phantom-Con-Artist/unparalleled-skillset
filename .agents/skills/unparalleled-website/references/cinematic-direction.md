# Cinematic direction with a human hand

Cinematic means controlling attention over time. It does not mean adding dark gradients, film grain, frosted panels, giant headings, or neon rim lights to every brief. Keep the five craft dimensions, but derive their expression from the product.

| Dimension | Product-driven question | Decision to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | What real condition reveals the product or its operation? | Source, direction, visibility, and purpose; diffuse daylight or no lighting effect may be right. |
| Color | Which observed pigments or existing identity cues belong here? | Evidence and semantic roles rather than film-stock or genre presets. |
| Atmosphere | What should the visitor sense about the environment? | Useful material cues; no obligatory noise, haze, or gradients. |
| Typography | How does this product speak and what must people read? | Distinctive, available, readable type rather than generic sans or magazine formulas. |
| Spatial blend | Which relationship becomes clearer through depth? | A meaningful 2D/3D transition with a static equivalent. |

## Discreet imperfection

Allow a small, intentional irregularity when it communicates something true: a hand-drawn route on a precise map, an offset note aligned with an actual operation, or timing that follows a material's resistance. Name the intent in the rationale. A drawn annotation is a mark, not a handwriting typeface for copy; reserve handwriting faces for briefs that ask for handwritten work ([brief-vocabulary.md](brief-vocabulary.md)). Do not distribute fake randomness everywhere or turn “human” into another distressed texture preset.

A deviation earns its place when it strengthens the subject, stays readable, and survives responsive layout. Keep measured alignment where the reader depends on it, including forms, controls, numerical comparisons, and focus order. Clipping, accidental overflow, weak contrast, fake metrics, and broken interaction are defects, not character.

## Finish without perfectionism

Set the product message, visual thesis, primary action, and verification criteria before polishing. Make the first review about story and functionality, then one focused craft pass about type, color, rhythm, and meaningful motion. Stop when the thesis is visible and the checks pass. Further changes need a named problem they solve, not a feeling that every surface could be smoother.
