# 3D website references: spatial intent and inspection limits

Use when researching a 3D/hybrid public experience, alongside [reference-intentions.md](reference-intentions.md) and [pipeline-3d.md](pipeline-3d.md). These are examples to study, not styles, assets or implementations to copy. Inspection date: 27 October 2026. A reachable URL, screenshot, creator account and fully tested interaction are different evidence. Neither example certifies accessibility or performance for a new project.

## Igloo Inc.

Reference: [igloo.inc](https://www.igloo.inc/). The [makers' case study](https://www.awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html) describes spatial journey planning, product enclosures, transitions and browser iteration. That account is evidence of creator intent, not a live audit of every scene.

A fresh browser capture reached a gray startup state on desktop. Phone captures showed a centered wireframe igloo-like enclosure with branching linework and small numeric annotations. The captures were opened; the complete product journey, loading completion, keyboard path and reduced-motion behavior were not verified. Do not claim that a clean DOM report proves scene quality: a canvas can contain the entire experience while DOM heuristics see very little.

Study how an object, its enclosure and the transitions between moments can follow one material/spatial rule. For a new product, derive a different rule from its own mechanism. Prototype the hardest camera/object/DOM relationship and its fallback before polishing. Do not transplant ice, wireframes, particles, the logo, opening delays or camera choreography as a universal “3D website” treatment.

## Algal Biotechnology Laboratory: user-supplied demo

Reference: [algal-biotech-lab.pages.dev](https://algal-biotech-lab.pages.dev/). The user identifies this as their demo. A desktop browser screenshot was captured and opened; the text-only fetch tool could not access it. The full-page capture subsequently timed out, so the complete scroll journey, phone layout and functional controls were not verified in this pass.

The observed opening combines a translucent green cell-like object with fine structural detail, a right-side index naming cell parts, a small scale annotation, research-oriented navigation and a concrete laboratory introduction. The visible prompt connects scrolling to taking the cell apart. This suggests a product-specific relationship between scientific subject, spatial decomposition, explanatory labels and reading. The claimed scroll transformation itself remains unverified; visible scientific statements and measurement accuracy were not independently validated.

Study the intent: make the subject the explanatory object, connect labels to meaningful parts, and let a spatial operation clarify the research. A different product should supply its own real object/process, scale, labels and evidence. Do not copy the cell, laboratory identity, pale-green palette, typography, text, anatomy or claims into another project. A service may need a truthful process model rather than an invented physical product.

## How to use a reference during the design loop

In the prospective direction record, separate **observed detail**, **interpretation**, **new product relationship**, **prototype test** and **unverified limit**. Discuss the interpretation with the user, build one representative specimen, and iterate through [design-collaboration.md](design-collaboration.md). A reference link or cinematic still is not the specimen and not approval for development.

When revisiting either site, inspect the opening, a meaningful mid-sequence, a return/reverse interaction and the action endpoint at desktop and phone widths. Record input, motion and loading limits honestly. Supplement stalled scenes with creator accounts, but never describe inferred behavior as tested. Keep the complete semantic, native-scroll reading path, keyboard/touch controls, actual contrast and reduced-motion route in the new work even when a reference's treatment differs.
