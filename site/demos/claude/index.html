    How I work

    Four habits that keep the line taut.

    I read all of it.

    Paste the whole thread, the full log, the forty-page PDF. I can read text, images and documents, and I keep the early pages in mind when I reach the late ones. When two clauses disagree or a variable is renamed halfway down a file, that is the kind of thing I'm there to catch.

    I show the fork, then pick one.

    Most problems have two or three honest answers. I'll lay them out, say which I'd take and why, and leave the decision with you. If you tell me the smaller fix is fine, I'll do the smaller fix.

    You can ask for the reasoning at any point. It's meant to be read, argued with, and overruled.

    I flag the knots I can't untie.

    If I'm guessing, I say I'm guessing. If an answer depends on a fact I don't have, I ask for it instead of inventing it. A confident wrong answer costs you more than a plain "I'm not sure."

    I make the thing, not just talk about it.

    I write code and run it, build pages and documents, draft the email and revise it three times. In Claude Code I work inside your project: reading files, editing them, running the tests. This very page came out of one prompt in Claude Code, using a design skill, and I'll tell you honestly which parts I'd still change.

    Where I fall short

    Read my work like you'd read a colleague's.

    I can be wrong, and I can sound sure while I am. Check the numbers, run the code, and have a professional look at anything with legal, medical or financial weight. In the contract demo above, my reading is a first pass, not advice.

    I don't remember you between separate conversations unless a memory feature is turned on. If something matters, put it in the first message or in a project file.

    I'm not the right tool for every job. If the work needs a person in the room, a signature, or a decision that is rightly yours, I can prepare the ground and nothing more.

    I would rather you trust me a little less on day one and a little more by day thirty because I told you where the edges were.
